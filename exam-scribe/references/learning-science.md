# Why the material looks the way it does

Use this to explain the design to a user, or to make judgement calls that fit it.

## The core finding

In a large review of study techniques (Dunlosky et al., 2013), **practice testing** and **distributed
(spaced) practice** were rated the most useful; **summarising, highlighting and re-reading** the least.
Polished summaries also create a *fluency illusion*: reading them feels like knowing. So ExamScribe
builds notes that make the student retrieve before they read, and schedules that retrieval over time.

## Principles and where they appear

| Principle | What it means | Where it shows up |
|---|---|---|
| Retrieval practice | answering from memory strengthens memory more than re-reading | recall mode (answers blurred until tried), self-tests, flashcards, review sheets |
| Pretesting | trying questions before studying improves later learning, even when the guesses are wrong | 3-question pretest at the top of each chapter |
| Spacing | reviews spread over days beat one long session | study calendar: reviews at +1, +3, +7, +14, +30 days |
| Successive relearning | re-test missed items until answered correctly several times | mistake log: missed questions return until right 3 times in a row |
| Interleaving | mixing topics trains choosing the right method, not just executing it | weekly mixed practice sets and the mock exam |
| Advance organisers | an overview first gives details a place to attach | course map, chapter "big picture", learning objectives at each section |
| Worked and faded examples | study a full solution, then complete a partly solved one | each worked example is followed by a "your turn" copy with the last steps hidden |
| Elaboration | asking "why?" deepens understanding | `why:` fields, strategy tables, explanations of each wrong mcq option |
| Discrimination / contrast | comparing confusable ideas side by side prevents mix-ups | comparison tables, "traps" page, misconception fields |
| Dual coding | words plus pictures | the book's own figures cropped into the notes, concept maps |
| Refutation | stating a misconception and correcting it changes wrong beliefs | `misconception: Wrong ... | Right ...` |
| Testing under exam conditions | practice in the exam's format and time pressure | timed mock exam matching the exam's formats |

## Why accuracy matters more than polish

A wrong fact in study notes is worse than a missing one: the student rehearses it until it is fluent. That is
why every claim carries evidence, why answer keys are re-solved, and why uncertainty is shown instead of smoothed
over. When in doubt, prefer a shorter, fully cited note over a longer, partly supported one.

## A study routine to recommend

1. New chapter: pretest (5 min) -> read the notes in read mode -> self-test in recall mode.
2. Review days: flashcards, then the review sheet with answers hidden; redo missed questions.
3. Weekly: one mixed practice set; export results and log them (`mistakes import`).
4. Two days before the exam: the timed mock exam. The day before: cheat sheet and traps, light flashcards.
