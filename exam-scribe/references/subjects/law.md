# Subject profile: law

- **Priorities:** rules and their elements, exceptions and defences, leading cases with their holdings,
  statutory wording, and the tests courts apply.
- **Rules:** use a `rule` block: `rule:` (the rule as the book states it), one `element:` per element,
  `exception:` lines, and `case:` lines (case name and what it decided), each cited.
- **Wording matters:** statutory and doctrinal language must be quoted, not paraphrased, in `rule:`
  (a paraphrase can change the legal meaning). Keep "may"/"shall"/"must", "reasonable", "knowingly".
- **Jurisdiction:** note which jurisdiction a rule belongs to if the book says so; never import rules from
  another jurisdiction or from memory.
- **Essays and problem questions** (IRAC): `strategy` rows such as "facts show a promise without consideration
  -> check promissory estoppel"; `outline` blocks for likely essay prompts.
- **Questions:** mcq on which element is missing, true/false on exceptions, short "apply the rule to these
  facts" questions.
